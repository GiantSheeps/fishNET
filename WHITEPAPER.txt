fishNET - a hybrid eco-evolutionary model of marine fish populations
====================================================================

WHAT IT IS

fishNET is a spatially explicit model of marine fish populations that couples individual
fish, their genetics, and the ocean they live in within a single mass-conserving
framework. Its defining choice is a hybrid representation. In regions where individual
detail matters, fish are carried as tagged super-individuals - discrete agents with a
position, depth, age, sex, parentage, body composition and a diploid genome - that swim,
feed, starve, mature, spawn and die one at a time. Everywhere else the same populations
are carried as stage-structured biomass fields that track not just how much fish is in a
cell but the first four statistical moments of every heritable trait in it. Both
representations obey the same equations, and fish convert between them as they cross the
boundary: agents leaving a detailed region are aggregated into biomass, and biomass
entering one is resampled back into individuals with its trait mean and variance
reproduced exactly. This buys the behavioural and genetic realism of an individual-based
model at something closer to the cost of a biomass model, which is what makes a global
run with two dozen species tractable at all.

HOW A FISH WORKS

Every fish, whether an agent or a biomass class, runs the same bioenergetics. Maximum
intake scales with body mass, respiration scales with mass and activity, and the surplus
fills a reserve before going to structural growth or to gonads. Feeding is a multi-prey
Holling type II response over plankton and over other fish, weighted by a diet matrix
and by a log-normal prey-to-predator size window, with fish prey gape-limited. Fish die
from size-dependent natural mortality, larval and density-dependent juvenile mortality,
thermal stress, oxygen stress, predation, starvation when their reserve runs out,
senescence, and fishing under a logistic size selectivity. Because every loss is
attributed to a cause, the model reports not only who died but what killed them, and the
breeding values of the dead - which is what allows selection to be measured rather than
assumed.

MOVEMENT, BEHAVIOUR AND EVOLUTION

Fish do not follow prescribed migration routes. Each decision, a fish weighs hunger,
local predation risk, thermal discomfort, isolation, light and pre-spawning ripeness,
and chooses a behaviour - forage, flee, seek better temperature, school, hide, or head
for a spawning site. The weights it places on those drives are themselves heritable
traits, so foraging boldness, thermal preference, schooling tendency and site fidelity
all evolve rather than being fixed by the modeller. Genetics are multilocus: unlinked
diploid loci, free recombination, per-locus mutation, and genetic correlations that
emerge from shared loci rather than being imposed. Mating is local by default, so gene
flow is limited by dispersal and regional races can form, and a heritable shift in
spawning date lets populations diverge in when they breed as well as where. Spawning
location is emergent - ripe adults home toward their own birthplace and toward
conspecific aggregations, eggs are released where the parents are, and currents carry
them from there.

THE OCEAN

The physical setting is prescribed rather than simulated. fishNET can run on an
idealised analytic basin, on an eddying shallow-water ocean, or on real ocean reanalysis
read from netCDF - temperature and currents on a depth-resolved grid, with bathymetry
and partial bottom cells so that shelves, slopes and the sea floor are real features
that demersal species are pinned to. Biogeochemistry is an internal NPZD model extended
with oxygen, which gives each species a Deutsch-style metabolic index: the ratio of
oxygen supply to demand, which scales aerobic scope and imposes suffocation mortality
where it falls too low. Oxygen minimum zones therefore shape distributions directly.
Nitrogen is conserved to round-off through the whole coupled system, which is the
model's main internal check that nothing is being created or lost by the machinery
itself.

WHAT IT IS FOR

The goal is to derive where fish are and how much of them there is from mechanism rather
than from fitted statistical envelopes. A species' range in fishNET is not prescribed;
it is the outcome of temperature tolerance, oxygen, prey availability, bathymetry,
transport and the evolving movement behaviour of the fish themselves. That makes the
model suited to questions that a correlative distribution model cannot answer: how
ranges shift under a warming or deoxygenating ocean, whether a population adapts or
moves or collapses, how fishing pressure interacts with climate stress, how thermal
tolerance and life history evolve under selection, and how much carbon fish move from
the surface to depth. Because the same run produces biomass, catch, size structure,
spatial distribution and genetic change together, these questions can be asked of one
internally consistent system rather than of separate models.

CURRENT CONFIGURATION

The present setup carries 23 species spanning six functional groups - small pelagic
forage fish, continental shelf groundfish, large oceanic pelagics, benthic flatfish and
batoids, mesopelagic plankton feeders, and polar specialists - chosen to represent the
main pathways by which energy moves through the ocean rather than to be an inventory of
commercial stocks. Each species is given a broad starting region using named latitude,
longitude and depth sectors, but that placement constrains only where it begins:
afterwards fish move, drift, spawn and shift their distributions wherever conditions and
their own evolving behaviour take them. The global configuration runs on a smooth annual
climatology built from GLORYS ocean reanalysis, fitted as an annual plus semiannual
harmonic so that the seasonal cycle is continuous and loops seamlessly for multi-year
runs.

VALIDATION

fishNET ships a self-test suite covering conservation, positivity, transport, genetics,
aggregation round-trips, determinism and consistency between the individual and biomass
representations. Against the real world, runs are scored on spawning stock biomass,
catch, community size spectra and documented events such as stock collapses, using
observational data assembled from public sources: the RAM Legacy stock assessment
database, ICES assessment and trawl survey archives, tuna commission catch records
including spatially gridded catch, and occurrence records from OBIS and GBIF.

LIMITATIONS WORTH STATING

Species parameters are literature-plausible but not calibrated against stock
assessments, so absolute biomass should be treated as provisional until fitted. A looped
climatology contains no interannual variability, so it can reproduce a mean state and a
seasonal cycle but cannot match a specific year or a dated collapse; that requires
forcing from the full reanalysis record. Mesopelagic biomass, though probably the
largest fish biomass in the ocean, is known only to within about an order of magnitude,
so the lanternfish and bristlemouth components are order-of-magnitude estimates rather
than constrained quantities. And the ocean is prescribed: the fish do not feed back on
the physics.
